Let's now get into what it means for agents to work well given this structure that I've just shared about how agents work involving this combination of setting goals, planning and action to achieve those goals.
Trustworthy agents execute with goals, plans and actions aligned.
In other words, we want agents to have high GPA or goal plan and action alignment.
Let me elaborate on that a little bit more. How do we measure an agent's GPA? The first step is to set goals for the agent in our setting with data agents.
These goals will often refer to the quality of the final response, such as is it relevant, is it grounded in retrieve results, as well as sub goals that the agent might be expected to achieve along the way in terms of retrieving relevant results to answer the final query.
Once we have the goal specified, the next steps involve ensuring a good amount of alignment between goals and plans, plans and actions and actions and goals.
And we have a collection of evaluations that make a use of LLM judges to check the agent's GPA.
The first evaluation is called plan quality. Plan quality sits at the interface of goal and plan and it checks whether the agent's plan is a good plan for achieving its goals.
The plan need not be static. It might evolve over time as new information becomes available to the agent.
But the re-planning steps also need to be well justified given the information and observations that the agent has access to.
Next up is plan adherence, which sits at the interface of planning and actions. Plan adherence checks whether the agent's actions are in conformance with or adhering to its plan.
In other words, is the agent actually acting in the way that it has planned to act?
Divisions from the plan can be indicative of failure modes, as well which are helpful for the developer to be aware of and address as the developer is building data agents.
The third evaluation, also implemented with an LLM judge, is execution efficiency.
Execution efficiency sits at the interface between goal and action.
So it's in this portion of the Venn diagram and it checks whether the execution path that the agent took, the sequence of actions that it executed, was that the most efficient path for achieving the goal.
It can help identify redundancies in the agent's planning process, which are sources for improvement as we go through an iterative process for developing data agents.
Finally, logical consistency sits at the intersection of all three of our GPA pillars and it checks for inconsistencies between the plan and the goal.
For example, is the plan incorporating or suggesting steps that are inconsistent with achieving the goal or sub-goals, as well as inconsistencies between planning and re-planning steps or between planning and action steps, all of which are sources of incorrectness and opportunities for improvement of the data agent.
