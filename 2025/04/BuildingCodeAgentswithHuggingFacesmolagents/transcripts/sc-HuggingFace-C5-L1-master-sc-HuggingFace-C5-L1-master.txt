What is an agent?
This question has been debated for months, if not years, and no definition will be perfect.
The most rigorous that we found is this one, an AI agent is a program where an AI model
output impacts the execution flow.
In other words, the LLM is embedded as a brain in the computer program as a vehicle.
The LLM decisions then drive the vehicle towards the user expected goal, but the impact
of AI models on the execution flow can vary.
Mini-agentic is not a clear-cut one-zero concept.
There are levels of agency.
In the first, the lowest level, the LLM outputs simply controls the decision in the program
workflow.
The higher level of agency, the LLM can call an external tool.
At yet a higher level of agency, we will see the agent taking decisions on the next step
or the next iteration or even the stop condition of the program.
This is what's called the multi-step workflow.
Finally, at the highest level of agency, an agentic workflow can create or start another
agency workflow at a middle level.
This notion of levels of agency has been reflected in the evolution of agents' capabilities
throughout the years.
As the release of the first LLM's, more and more agency has been unlocked for AI agents,
which went from simple routers where the LLM output mostly decided toward which branch
of an IFL switch the program should go to tool calling and multi-step agents.
And taking a step back, the highest level of agency that we can give an LLM is to let
them write and execute code.
This is what we call content agents, and it's a bread and butter of the small agent library
that we'll cover in details in the next lesson of this short course.
But let's continue on our history of agents.

increasingly suited to power multi-step agents with longer time horizons.
